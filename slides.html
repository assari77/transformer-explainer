How Do AI Models Like ChatGPT Actually Work?

Behind all the magic of modern AI is a single core invention called the Transformer — and its job is surprisingly simple: predicting the very next word.

What It Looks Like

You give the AI an unfinished sentence:

"The cat sat on the ___"

What the Transformer Does

It calculates probabilities for every word it knows and picks the best next word:

→ "warm" (55.1%) or "mat" (22.4%)

Slide 2 of 10 · The 3-Step Pipeline

The 3 Building Blocks of Our App

Every time the Transformer predicts a word, your sentence travels from left to right through the 3 panels on screen:

1. EMBEDDING

Splits your sentence into individual word tokens and turns each word into a list of numbers (colors).

2. TRANSFORMER

Connects words together using Self-Attention (context) and a Neural Network (MLP).

3. PROBABILITIES

Converts the final numbers into percentage scores and selects the winning next word.

Key Takeaway for Beginners

Text In → Numbers → Context & Thinking → Probabilities → Next Word Out.

Slide 3 of 10 · Panel 1: Embedding

Step 1: Turning Words Into Numbers (Embeddings)

Computers cannot multiply or add letters like "c-a-t". First, the model splits the sentence into Tokens and looks up a numerical list (a Vector) for each word.

What Are Tokens?

  • Each word is numbered in order: #0, #1, #2...
  • The Token Counter under the input box shows how many tokens are in your sentence.

What Are the Colored Squares?

  • Each word becomes 8 numbers between -1 and +1.
  • Blue = Positive (+1), White = Zero (0), Orange = Negative (-1).
  • Words with similar roles share similar number patterns!

Slide 4 of 10 · Panel 1: Position Matters

Why It Says "TOKEN + POS" (Position)

Word order changes everything: "The dog bit the man" means something very different from "The man bit the dog"!

Word Meaning + Spot in Line

To make sure the Transformer knows word order, it adds a Positional Encoding (POS) to each word's numbers based on whether it is at spot #0, #1, or #4.

Spot the Difference!

In "The cat sat on the", compare #0 The and #4 the:
Same word = same overall color pattern, but slightly different shades because one is at position #0 and one is at position #4!

Slide 5 of 10 · Panel 2: Self-Attention

Step 2A: Masked Self-Attention (Finding Clues)

How does the model know what you feed in "I have a parrot. Every morning I feed my ___"? Through Self-Attention!

Looking Back for Clues

  • The last word ("my") looks back at all earlier words in the sentence.
  • Orange curves show which earlier words it connects to.
  • Thicker curve = Stronger attention!

Why "Masked"?

  • When predicting the next word, the model can only look at past words, never future words.
  • It matches a Query (Q) ("what am I looking for after feed my?") with a Key (K) ("I am a parrot!").

Slide 6 of 10 · Context Window

Context Window: How Much Can AI Remember?

Every AI model has a limited working memory called its Context Window. Words that fall outside the window are completely forgotten!

Window = 10 Tokens (In Memory)

All 9 words of "I have a parrot. Every morning I feed my" fit inside memory.

The model remembers your parrot → Predicts "parrot".

Window = 5 Tokens (Forgotten!)

Only "Every morning I feed my" fits in memory. "I have a parrot." is crossed out as FORGOTTEN.

The model forgets your parrot → Reverts to "cat"!

Slide 7 of 10 · Panel 2: Feed-Forward MLP

Step 2B: Feed-Forward MLP (The Brain's Knowledge)

After Self-Attention gathers context from the sentence, the signal flows into the Feed-Forward MLP box in the middle of Block 2.

Self-Attention vs. MLP

  • Self-Attention looks sideways at other words in your prompt (e.g., finding "parrot").
  • Feed-Forward MLP is a Neural Network that recalls world facts learned during training (e.g., parrots eat seeds/fruit; cats sit on warm mats).

+ Residual & LayerNorm

  • Notice the dotted arch over the top (+ Residual & LayerNorm).
  • This is a "highway shortcut" so the original word signal is never lost while passing through the neural network.

Slide 8 of 10 · Panel 3: Probabilities

Step 3: Softmax & Next-Word Probabilities

A Transformer never just knows a single answer — it calculates a probability percentage for candidate words using a math function called Softmax.

What Softmax Does

Turns raw neural network scores (logits) into clean percentages between 0% and 100% that all add up to 100%.

Reading Panel 3

The top box shows the SELECTED NEXT TOKEN, and the bars below show the runners-up (e.g., parrot, bird, pet, cat, dog).

Tip

You can also click any candidate bar in 3. PROBABILITIES to manually select that word before appending!

Slide 9 of 10 · Temperature Control

Temperature (0.0 to 1.0): Focus vs. Creativity

Why does ChatGPT sometimes give the exact same answer, and other times give creative or surprising answers? Because of Temperature (T)!

T = 0.0 (Focused)

100% on the #1 word.
Always picks the single safest, most predictable word. Best for math, coding, and facts.

T = 0.5 (Balanced)

Natural spread.
Favors the top choice (~55%) while keeping realistic alternatives alive.

T = 1.0 (Creative)

Flattens the bars.
Gives #2, #3, and #4 words a big chance to win. Great for brainstorming & stories!

Slide 10 of 10 · The Generation Loop

How One Word Becomes a Whole Paragraph

If a Transformer only predicts one word at a time, how does it write a whole email or essay? By repeating the loop!

The Autoregressive Loop

  • Pass 1: "Data visualization empowers users to" → picks "explore"
  • Pass 2: "... users to explore" → picks "complex"
  • Pass 3: "... to explore complex" → picks "models"

Summary: You Now Understand Transformers!

  • Context Window & Self-Attention: Connecting clues in memory
  • MLP + Softmax + Temperature: Scoring and picking the next word
